design directions · grenn pwomennen

Three skins, one body.

Each direction below is a complete system — ground, ink, accents, and three type voices (display / body / typed label) — applied to the site's real words. All three come from the material world of the practice: coffee-processed film, phytogram chemistry, indigo dye. Scroll through and notice which one feels like the work.

direction 01 — recommended

KAFE — coffee-processed film

From Katherine: film re-processed in coffee. Warm blacks like a darkroom tray, kraft-paper bone, oxidized red instead of bright red. Type: Fraunces (a soft, slightly wonky serif with ink-traps — it already looks printed by hand) for display, Alegreya for reading, Courier Prime for labels — the typewriter of field notes and film-can stickers.

direction 02

FITOGRAM — plant chemistry on emulsion

From The Marks Remember: phytograms — leaves laid on film, plant chemistry eating the emulsion. Green-blacks, chlorophyll, rust, one acid-amber. Type: Cormorant Garamond (thin, fragile, almost botanical) roughened by the filter so its edges are eaten like emulsion, EB Garamond body, Cutive Mono — an older, rougher typewriter — for labels.

direction 03

ENDIGO — dye vat, night sea

The crossing palette: indigo — an organic dye with its own Caribbean plantation history — unbleached cotton, madder red, raw sisal. The coldest of the three, but cold like open water at night, not like a screen. Type stays closest to the current build: EB Garamond carrying both display and body, Courier Prime labels.

How the type gets its texture

Five treatments, all live CSS/SVG — no images, no gloss. Each is tied to a moment in the experience, never used everywhere at once. (The roughen filters render in Chrome and Firefox; Safari falls back to clean edges — acceptable degradation, the grain layer still carries texture there.)

01 — eaten edges · svg displacement

yon fenèt kite louvri

An SVG turbulence filter displaces the letterforms' edges — ink eaten by wet paper, emulsion eaten by plant chemistry. For: chamber titles, the proverb, Grann's key line.

02 — ink bleed · layered shadow

ou antre.

A tight halo plus one warm, offset blur — the letter pressed slightly too hard into soft paper. For: RESPÈ, arrival words, anything that lands.

03 — the stamp · typewriter caps, roughened

tijuana · 3,412 km · travay

Labels stop being UI and become archive markings — the stamp on a film can, the customs mark in a passport. Replaces every Space Grotesk label in the current build.

04 — unstable baseline · letters sit wrong

Grenn Pwomennen

Every nth letter sits a hair high or low, rotated a fraction — hand-set type, a worn press. Subtle enough to feel rather than see. For: the name, one or two display moments only.

05 — torn underline · clip-path

kite yon souvni

Underlines are torn strips, not rules. For: links and the offering button — the only two interactive text forms in the site.

My recommendation

KAFE as the body of the site, with FITOGRAM's chlorophyll green adopted as the fifth color (replacing KAFE's moss) — the darkroom warmth of coffee-processed film everywhere, with the plant-chemistry green reserved for one thing only: the sous stars, the wellsprings. Living green for the places that send life outward.

Type: Fraunces for display (its ink-traps and slight wonk are organic at the letterform level — texture that survives even where SVG filters don't), Alegreya for reading, Courier Prime for every label — the entire "UI voice" becomes typewriter, and the interface starts reading as an annotated archive rather than an app.

ENDIGO doesn't die: it becomes the chamber palette for crossing stories — when a fragment is about water and passage (Nassau, Miami by boat, the kanntè), the chamber cools into indigo. The site's ground stays coffee; the sea appears only when the story enters it.
